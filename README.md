# Nanovore

Microscopic strategy inside a body, inspired by Eufloria. Command craft, scout living cells, establish factories, and expand. All three factions start on a 128-cell map. Fighters prefer units; bombers prefer factories. A slider sets their production ratio. Destroy enemy factories to win.

Normal matches start with an owned home cell and factories at the start of growth. The starting forces are 5 Immune fighters, 10 Virus fighters, and 25 Fungal spores. Capture an undefended cell with 1 Immune fighter, 2 Virus fighters, or 5 Fungal spores. The full group must reach the cell and complete landing before any units are spent. Extra units remain. A new move order, a missing member, or a defender stops landing progress.

Virus and Immune bombers can seed a cell without being spent. A visible seed falls from the craft into the cell over 1.5 seconds. A defender or another capture cancels the seed. A launched seed can finish after the bomber dies, including when it is the faction's last unit. The craft waits eight seconds before another seed launch. Virus uses one ready bomb and regrows it with the existing payload timer. Immune bombers also launch one new fighter every 12 seconds while free to act. These fighters cost no cell nutrients and follow the carrier's current order. Full unit storage holds a ready launch until a slot becomes free. Capture costs are independent of combat stats.

Nutrients are local to each cell. Factories collect them from the cell's availability and production rate into storage capped at 180. Unit jobs pay their cost when scheduled. Repairs also spend nutrients and stop unit production during repair. Empty storage can delay new jobs. Cells do not transfer nutrients, and there is no player control for collection or transfer. This change keeps that economy. In diagnostic state output, `energy` is the stored amount and `nutrients` is the availability multiplier.

The current small combat changes raise Immune fighter speed from 2.6 to 2.8, increase the Virus fighter shot interval from 0.6 to 0.66 seconds, and raise Fungal burst damage from 3 to 3.6 and cloud damage from 1 to 1.2 per second. In 18 controlled fights at the 1:2:5 ratios, Virus still won against both sides and Immune still won against Fungus. These small changes have not made the ratios equal in combat. Capture costs stay fixed. Run `python3 tests/e2e/starting_force_balance.py build/windows-cross/nanovore.exe --output build/colonization_review/duels --seeds 3` to repeat the measurement.

Virus forces use large swarms of light craft and spike projectiles. Immune forces use fewer, tougher craft that stun a target with a blob, devour it, then heal nearby allies. The fungal trial fields swarms of melee spores that burst into toxic clouds. Immune fighters take 2 seconds to manufacture and bombers take 2.5 seconds before factory-size and AI modifiers. Immune factory trunks have 5,760 health and withstand 96 direct hits; branches have 3,600 health and withstand 64 direct hits. Small factories scale health and manufacture rate by area.

Virus fighters and bombers use narrow hulls with flat facets, swept fins, and pointed ends. Bombs are closed darts. Detachable spikes are tapered needles. The attached spike pattern stretches along the hull; this is a visual setting and does not change combat. Each hull has 72 triangles; each reusable spike retains 48. Edit `virus_ships` in `assets/models/source/cell-unit-factory-shape.json`. Run `blender --background --factory-startup -noaudio --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/make_biology.py -- assets/models --virus-only` to regenerate these four meshes and `source/virus_shapes.blend`.

Immune factories use fused rounded masses and broad bulges. Virus factory tips are slender bodies with many fine spikes. Fungal factories use brown curved stems that darken with distance along their growth paths. Each tip has three rounded tendrils in separate angular sectors. Wider forks and reduced jitter give neighboring ends more room. The editable shapes are in `assets/models/source/cell-unit-factory-shape.json`. Run `blender --background --factory-startup -noaudio --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/make_biology.py -- assets/models --factories-only` to regenerate the factory meshes and `factory_shapes.blend`.

Seeded membranes, organelles, branching factories, and wet anatomical backgrounds form the world. Units grow at factory tips. Damaged limbs break away. Fog returns gradually and strongly blurs unseen areas. Direct ray tracing provides light, shadows, and atmospheric scattering. Colors come from vertices; each frame uses current data only.

Each visible organelle supports a factory. Large organelles support branching factories. Smaller organelles support wider trunks, one short child branch, and small heads that also produce units. `shared.branches.additional_root_scale` sets their maximum size relative to a large factory; organelle radius reduces it further. `additional_root_width_scale` sets small-trunk width without changing head size, health, or manufacture rate. `additional_root_branch` adds one child per small root. Navigation bounds follow the visible stem and head widths. Small-factory health and manufacture rates scale with cross-sectional area. Fungal sources share the existing cell output limit. `growth_meshes.rounded` preserves round stem sections along short curve spans. `bulges` sets the number of broad width changes along each limb; zero keeps a uniform profile. `glow_scale` and `tint_strength` control factory light and faction tint. Optional `length_color` sets a target color, distance, and strength for a gradual color change along connected stems. Factory `setup_speed` scales sprouting, establishment, branch growth, and root start delays. Immune uses 2.0, Fungus uses 0.9, and Virus uses the default 1.0. A higher value reduces growth time. Unit manufacture uses its separate duration.

Textures are permitted on any object when the game generates them. Noise and other procedural methods may generate color, normal, roughness, opacity, emission, and environment maps. Vertex colors remain supported.

Simulation rules remain separate from rendering. The project uses direct functions and contiguous data, with real application checks.

Every faction choice uses the generated map `large_level.json`; the start menu or `--faction virus|immune|fungus` selects your side. Each new game seed rotates the home triangle on a field with a radius of 120 units and deals the three homes to the factions at random. Starting forces (`match.starts[].forces`) form a ring above each home. The other cells use seeded, relaxed Voronoi sites. Larger regions receive larger cells. Every pair retains a gap of at least four units. Older two-side levels remain diagnostic fixtures. Every intact, mature factory head produces units, including heads below forks. Stems extend through the cell wall. All factions grow factory trunks roughly from the nucleus toward each organelle center. Large factories use two fork levels with shorter, thinner child limbs. Seeded bends and split angles give each tree a different shape. Factory paths use unequal S-bends with smaller seeded bends at three scales. Virus is nearly straight, Immune has moderate curves, and Fungus has strong curves. `branches.tree.curvature` controls the bend scale; `curve_jitter` controls variation in lobe size, bend position, and fine detail. Growth, navigation, and breakage follow the same sampled path; damage still removes one of four sections. Fungal output stays within the existing cell limit.

Faction motion and sound use the profiles in `assets/presentation/*_personality.json`. Virus turns in short steps and uses crackling sounds. Immune rolls slowly and uses paired command tones. Fungus follows changing local goals and uses soft granular sounds. Direct movement orders keep their target. Each profile also sets the motion rate and amount for organelles and factories. `sound_bank` links the profile to a named bank in `assets/audio/audio.json`. Run `python3 scripts/make_sfx.py` to rebuild the procedural sound files from `sound-synthesis.json`.

Enemy craft that become visible near an owned cell create a territory alert. The first contact by each enemy faction creates one alert per cell ownership period. The three oldest alerts appear at the top right. Further alerts stay in the queue. Click a tile to move the camera and select the cell. Click X to dismiss it without moving. Alerts stay until the player reads or dismisses them. `assets/presentation/communications.json` sets the territory margin, contact range, camera focus distance, and message timing.

Enemy messages appear at the bottom right. Each faction has separate lines for first contact with player ships and cells. It also has one line for each control threshold from 10 through 90 cells. A threshold runs once per match, including when ownership starts above that threshold. Messages play in order, with a seven-second display and a three-second gap. The profiles hold the text; the game does not select behavior by faction name.

Performance: `python3 tests/e2e/performance.py build/windows-cross/nanovore.exe` runs the two perf scenes on `assets/levels/performance_battle.json`: 122 developed cells, storage 80% full so production continues, Immune and Fungus attacking the Virus-held center while Virus colonizes six neutral cells nearby, with sight and fog on. After 1,800 battle ticks it renders at maximum zoom-out and maximum zoom-in. It prints CPU cost per simulation tick by phase, and each F1 profiler span in ms and percent of frame. Logs, captures and `report.json` go to `build/performance/`.

The primary stress scene is `assets/levels/three_faction_stress.json`: five fully developed cells and 60 seconds of actual production per faction, with all armies and reinforcements ordered to the center. Preparation runs 3,600 normal production steps with full initial nutrient storage and no combat or unit expiry. The produced armies assemble at their front cells. Counts follow faction production; unit storage holds 4,096. Run it with `python3 scripts/proton_run.py build/windows-cross/nanovore.exe --world assets/levels/three_faction_stress.json`.

C++ and Vulkan target Windows. Linux builds use MinGW, `glslc`, and an initialized Steam Proton 11 prefix:

```sh
bash scripts/check.sh
python3 scripts/proton_run.py build/windows-cross/nanovore.exe
```
The larger CPU stress scene is `assets/levels/territory_stress.json`. It uses 128 mature cells, with 41 Virus, 46 Immune, and 41 Fungal cells. It prepares ten seconds of actual production. Longer preparation can exceed the 4,096-unit storage capacity and must fail at startup. Run `python3 tests/e2e/combat_perf.py build/windows-cross/nanovore.exe --world assets/levels/territory_stress.json --ticks 1800 --repeats 3`. This measures CPU simulation; it does not measure rendered frame rate. `communications` and `faction_motion` are real-application CTest groups for the new behavior.

The CPU suite keeps small scenes for input, combat, and asset validation. Those scenes check active mechanisms; they do not define normal map size or faction balance. The old axial factory baselines, fixed two-faction startup checks, and historical renderer comparison tools have been removed. Current map checks use 128 cells. `three_factions` also checks production at the 4,096-unit capacity on the 128-cell stress scene. The opt-in `map_render` CTest group renders all 128 cells at native 1080p with default sampling, checks lighting storage and cache reuse, and saves the capture. Radiance cascades retain lighting history, so render checks compare simulation and scene input where needed and do not require fresh-process pixel equality.
